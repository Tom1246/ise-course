# ISE-A1 · Problem and Stakeholder Evidence Brief

**This brief argues one thing: checking a postgraduate course timetable by eye goes wrong easily, and it is worth spending one semester on a small tool.**


What this project will do: take a course-registration plan and the credit constraints of the programme requirements as input, decide conflicts automatically, and output a conflict-free timetable suggestion plus a justified order to register in, with a target of finishing **within 5 minutes**.

## 0. Background

I am a 2026-entry master's student at UCAS, and in my first year I take centralised teaching courses at the Yuquan Road campus. The university rules that students in centralised teaching cannot register for courses across campuses, and at the same time they must meet the total-credit and degree-course-count requirements of the programme requirements.

Planning one semester's courses means doing three things at once: picking candidate courses out of the official course-planning workbook issued by the academic affairs office; copying down "weekday + period + teaching week" for each course from the public course listing (jwba); and then arranging them into a weekly timetable to see whether anything clashes. The hard parts are: **the course-planning workbook has no times and no teaching weeks**; "does this count as a clash" needs three conditions to hold at once; and many required courses run in the autumn semester only, and once a section fills up you have to switch sections — **and the whole time skeleton of the timetable is rearranged with it**. Checking it by hand takes about 30 minutes, and **both the version I arranged by hand and the version the AI arranged went wrong**.

**The flow I will build**:

1. First read the programme requirements, and fix how many credits this semester has to cover and which degree courses must be taken;
2. Copy the candidate courses out of the course-planning workbook into a table of my own — but that table **has no teaching weeks and no weekday/period**, so the times have to come from another step;
3. For each course, go to the "time and place" page of the public course listing (jwba) and note down the **weekday + period + teaching week**;
4. Put these into the weekly timetable and check cell by cell whether anything clashes — deciding "does this count as a clash" must look at weekday, period and teaching week **at the same time**, and note which courses run in only one semester (miss it and you wait a year);

5. Register in order inside the window; during registration you may hit "the section I want is already full" and have to switch sections — **once a section changes, every arrangement for the course changes**;
6. For anything not obtained, or where you were forced to switch sections, go back to step 4 and re-check the whole table.

**What happens if this goes badly**: if the semester is not planned properly, the shortfall has to be made up in later semesters. So once something goes wrong inside the window (section full, autumn-only), it is not "one course fewer" — it is that **the timing of the whole timetable has to be rearranged**.

## 2. Problem / value statement

### 2.1 Problems in the workflow
With the same set of courses on the same campus, I arranged one version each in two independent ways (a manual version and an AI version), and **both exposed problems**:

| | Manual version (S3, arranged by me by hand) | AI version (S4, arranged from the official timetable) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data it relied on | The course-library snapshot (its time fields were measured to disagree with the official pages) | The official academic "time and place" pages (the AI referred to the course-library snapshot's teaching weeks part-way through) |
| Size | 14 courses | 10 courses |
| Conclusion when first produced | Looked workable | **Zero conflicts** after checking against official data |
| **Pitfalls hit** | 1. **3 courses are placed in the same slot** (Thursday periods 10–12), mutually exclusive — found only after the plan was finished. 2. Adjusting course clashes by hand costs a great deal of effort | **While laying out the plan the AI used the teaching weeks from the course-library snapshot to make its registration recommendation, and its understanding of those courses was itself wrong**: Marine Acoustics, "every Sunday", is in fact week 3 only; the Saturday classes of Pattern Recognition and Dialectics of Nature are in fact week 6 only. The AI did not consider how courses are arranged across different teaching weeks and applied a one-vote veto (treating every week as if the course met at that time) |
| Lesson | Arranging a timetable by hand relies on the eye, and a clash is not necessarily visible | **Whatever the AI returns must be checked line by line against the original source** — its mistake is not miscalculation, it is that **its picture of how the courses are arranged may simply be wrong** |

**Registration of the required courses also went wrong.** I planned to register the required courses in an orderly way, and only found out when I entered the system:

- **Problem 1: I had not fixed the order to register in in advance, and by the time I came to register for the important courses the section I wanted was already full.** All **4 sections of Dialectics of Nature were full or even over capacity**; of the **8 sections of Theory and Practice of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era, 4 were full at 265/265** (-01 was 263/265, -06/-07 were 169/170, and only -08 had 42 places left); for Academic Ethics and Academic Writing — Sub-track only section 3 was left. Because these courses run in the autumn only, they cannot be left to the spring to make up, and missing them means waiting a year.
- **Problem 2: the important courses have to be taken, so I could only switch to sections that still had places, and once a section changes the arrangement of the whole timetable changes.** I went looking for sections with places left, so: Dialectics of Nature moved from the planned **Tuesday period 1 (+ Sunday period 1)** to **Wednesday period 5 (+ Saturday period 5)**, the New Era course from **Tuesday period 1** to **Wednesday period 1**, and the section of Academic Ethics and Academic Writing — General track originally on **Wednesday/Saturday period 7** was replaced.
- **Problem 3**: arranging a timetable by hand relies on the eye, and a clash cannot be seen. And once a required course moves, the whole time skeleton of the timetable changes, so time has to be spent arranging it all over again.

### 2.2 Value statement

The value of this project is that it turns "a person has to arrange a semester's timetable with their eyes across several sources" into "**one course-registration plan as input plus the credit constraints of the programme requirements → decide conflicts automatically → one conflict-free timetable + one justified order to register in**". Specifically:

1. **No more deciding conflicts by eye** — the decision rule is hard-coded as three conditions holding at once; and every "this course is rejected" states clearly **which weeks overlap**.
2. **The order to register in has a basis** — "how many seats are left" and "does it offered in only one semester" are combined into a single order, rather than going on feel.
3. **Changing a plan means re-running, not re-checking the whole table** — this cuts my workload enormously.

**Measurable**: baseline: **about 30 minutes per round as measured by me** (changing the registration by hand and then checking one plan completely through) → target: **course planning time < 5 minutes**; the designed timetable has no conflicts, and it can suggest priorities in the order to register in.

**Who benefits**: mainly me, twice per academic year; classmates on the same campus under the same rules can reuse the same project.

**Why this is worth a semester of software work rather than a cleverer spreadsheet**: the data sources and the current state of course enrolment **change every year**, and registration costs a great deal of time and effort over and over each semester, while doing it purely through an Excel spreadsheet still takes a very long time;

Neither of the two routes A and B above is enough to solve the problems in 2.1, so this is the route to take. ### 2.5 Boundaries for this semester: scope, non-goals, assumptions, stop conditions

- **Scope**: **one course-registration plan as input plus the credit constraints of the programme requirements → decide "weekday × period × teaching week" conflicts automatically → output a conflict-free timetable + a justified order to register in** (both official inputs in the input are frozen and archived first). On top of that: every rejection states clearly **which weeks overlap**; every course is labelled with its **semester taught**; and courses that run **in a single semester only and whose sections are already full** are listed separately.
- **Non-goals**: **no automatic registration**, this is registration advice only.
- **Assumptions**: the sources can still be obtained (so a frozen snapshot with a hash can be produced); teaching weeks are integer ranges and may have gaps; "courses that must be taken" are given by a person, not inferred by the tool.
- **Stop conditions**: if the semester of a course cannot be obtained from any source and cannot be recovered, **stop and report, do not assume it can be postponed**; if the public sources one day stop publishing teaching weeks/periods, narrow the scope to a "timetable consistency checker" rather than plugging the gap with guessed data.

## 4. Normal / boundary / misuse scenarios

### 4.1 Normal scenario: what it looks like when the system runs

**Input**: one course-registration plan + the credit constraints of the programme requirements — where the registration plan is assembled from two **official frozen snapshots** (course-planning workbook, 2026-08-28 version + public course listing, 2026-09-27 version), plus the "courses that must be taken" that I designate myself. Every run uses the same input, so the result is reproducible and traceable.

**The five steps and outputs of one normal run**:

1. **Assemble the input**: join the two sources by course code into the Yuquan Road subset (18th digit = `Y`; **732 raw records** for Yuquan Road in the course-planning workbook → **413 course codes** after de-duplication), then filter out by the programme requirements anything whose credits do not match, giving the candidate pool;
2. **Fill in the times**: for each course take **weekday + period + teaching week** from the "time and place" page of the public course listing (these two items are empty in the course-planning workbook, so this is the only way to fill them);
3. **Decide conflicts**: **same weekday ∩ period ranges intersect ∩ teaching weeks intersect**, all three holding at once counts as a conflict. For example, given the 9-15 plan as input, the system reports that Computer Algebra, Digital Image Processing and Analysis, and Deep Learning Methods and Applications **all collide on Thursday periods 10–12** and are **mutually exclusive, at most one can stay** — and states that the overlapping weeks are weeks 2–3 and weeks 6–16 (Deep Learning has no class in weeks 4–5, so it is not a continuous overlap across the whole semester);
4. **Order the registration**: sort on two dimensions, "**is it offered in only one semester**" and "**how many places are left in the section**". For example section 1 of the Sub-track is full at 170/170 and section 3 has 44/170 left, so section 3 goes first; autumn-only courses always rank ahead of other courses of the same period;
5. **Output**: one **conflict-free timetable** + one **separate order to register in**; every "this course is rejected" states **which weeks overlap**; every course is labelled with its **semester taught**.

**Criteria for a normal run**: re-running the same input gives the same conclusion (reproducible); every number can be traced back to its source and capture time (traceable); this round's time target is **< 5 minutes**, against about **30 minutes** for one manual round.

### 4.2 Boundary scenario:

- **Courses that neither timetable covers**: the public course listing has 59 extra and the course-planning workbook 11 extra, and **neither can be checked**. The system can only mark "**needs manual confirmation**".
- **Key fields in the course-planning workbook are empty**: teaching weeks and weekday/period are **0 records each**, and "enrolment limit" may also be **blank**. A blank can only be taken as "**unknown**", not as "unlimited" (only values shown as `/` are the official "no enrolment limit"); the time has to come from the other source, otherwise that single course cannot be checked at all.
- **The official numbers themselves go out of bounds**: values such as **422/420**, where the number enrolled exceeds the limit, do occur. The system can neither count it as "places still available" nor throw it away as dirty data — **it is precisely what shows that this course must be registered for**, and has to be handled separately.
- **Snapshots go stale**: the enrolled numbers crawled on 9-05 and those crawled on 9-15 are different (10 days apart). The system **cannot know by itself that the data is stale**, only by stating the **capture time** in the output; ordering from an old snapshot gets the order wrong.
- **Half-semester courses squeezed into the same cell**: weeks 11–18 are the New Era course, weeks 2–5 and 7–10 are Dialectics of Nature — they occupy the same cell in the weekly table and yet **do not count as a conflict**. This is exactly the easiest place to get it wrong: comparing "weekday + period" alone produces a **false positive**, and ignoring the teaching weeks produces a **miss**; and the teaching weeks themselves have gaps (such as 2–5, 7–17), so the rule has to be able to take in these discontinuous ranges. The system must report **the specific weeks that overlap**, otherwise it is impossible to judge whether it is acceptable.
- **Boundary of the code range**: courses whose 18th digit is `H` (Yanqi Lake) or `Z` (Zhongguancun) are **directly excluded** for me, which is a different matter from a "time conflict"; the two must be reported separately, with a reason for each.

### 4.3 Misuse scenarios: who might use it that way, what the consequences are and how to block it

- **Turning it into an automatic registration program**: it would break the fairness of first come, first served. The way to block it is to hard-code the boundary — the system only outputs advice and an order and **does not submit on anyone's behalf**.
- **Reading other people's registration records**: it would invade classmates' privacy. Only public sources are used (course-planning workbook + public course listing), and no personal registration data is touched.
- **Letting the AI invent section data and make a plan "look workable"**: this is exactly the pitfall I fell into this time (the AI version relied on wrong teaching weeks). Every section must be traceable to a **source + capture time**; if it does not match, raise an error and do not fill in a "plausible-looking" time.
- **Treating an old snapshot as "current"**: it would treat an already-full section as having places left. The time point must be stated in the output, and a stale one must be re-crawled.
- **Treating a blank enrolment number as "unlimited"**: the official "enrolment limit" **blank = unknown** (only values shown as `/` are "no enrolment limit"). Every blank is treated as "unknown", never as "choose freely".
