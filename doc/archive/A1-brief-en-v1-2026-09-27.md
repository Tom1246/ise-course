## 0. Scope statement

- **Workflow under study**: planning one semester of UCAS graduate courses at the 玉泉路 (Yuquanlu) campus,
  checking that the chosen combination has no timetable conflict, and deciding the order in which seats are
  taken inside the first-come-first-served selection window.
- **This brief claims**: producing a *single* input that can support both a conflict check and a grab-order
  ranking is impossible from any one source. It requires joining three heterogeneous sources by course code,
  and even after joining, a large share of courses still lacks one of the two field groups — including 6 of
  the 14 courses in the current plan whose semester is recorded nowhere. The repeat labour and the risk this
  creates justify a semester-sized intervention.
- **This brief does not claim**: that the selection system is defective; that automated tooling should replace
  the official submission procedure; or that the intervention covers graduation-audit rules.

## 2. Stakeholder and workflow map

- **User**: the author — the person executing the whole procedure each semester.
- **Affected parties**: classmates at the same campus under the same rules; the supervisor, whose research time
  is affected by where courses land.
- **Decision makers**: the offering departments and the academic affairs office — they publish the plan and set
  the rules, so a rule change reopens the whole problem.
- **Constraints**: Yuquanlu campus only (code position 18 = `Y`); no cross-campus selection; a floor on
  degree-course credits; first-come-first-served within a fixed window; every source is a point-in-time snapshot.
- **Current responsibility**: entirely manual, with no tool support.

```
1. read the degree-programme requirements        [nowhere in the data -- human input only]
2. assemble candidate sections + their times     [three sources, joined by hand -- E3, E4, E5]
3. collect capacity/enrolled per section         [only the public listing has it -- E4]
4. check for conflicts                           [BREAK POINT: three dimensions at once -- E1, E3, E4]
5. decide the order to take seats in             [second, different ordering problem -- E5, priority-rules.md]
6. submit inside the selection window            [first-come-first-served; failure forces a re-plan -- E2]
7. re-plan whatever failed                       [returns to step 4 over the whole grid -- E1]
```

**Largest break point**: steps 2–5 (E3 + E4 + E5). A clash needs same weekday **and** overlapping periods
**and** overlapping week ranges; the two official workbooks carry neither the week nor the weekday/period,
and the only source that carries them has no campus field, so building the input means joining sources that
overlap only partially.

## 3. Problem / value statement

**Who, where, how often.** Each selection season the author must assemble roughly a dozen courses into one
semester satisfying the programme's credit requirements, from a campus-restricted pool — 414 distinct Yuquanlu
courses recorded in the official workbooks, 347 in the public listing, and only 217 in both. The window
between publication and closing is a few days and the plan is revised repeatedly inside it.

**The pain, measured.** Two independent things make this manual:

1. **The fields are split across sources.** The 4,877-record official import carries campus, semester,
   attribute and credits, but for its 732 Yuquanlu records it carries the week range **0 times** and the
   weekday/period **0 times** — the importer says so itself, four times. The public listing carries the week
   range, weekday/period, capacity and enrolled count for all 347 of its Yuquanlu entries, but it has no
   campus field, so those 347 can only be identified through the course-code rule, and it records no semester.
   Joining the two by course code leaves 197 codes with no week/period/seats and 130 with no campus/semester.
   Of the 14 courses in the current plan, **6 have no semester in any source, while 8 of the 13 that have a
   capacity figure are at or above 90% full** — and 5 of those 6 unknown-semester courses are at or above 95%.
2. **The check itself is three-dimensional.** A clash requires same weekday, overlapping periods and
   overlapping week ranges together. The measured week occupancy of the plan ranges from 5 to 19 teaching
   weeks depending on the course, and in the 2026-09-05 plan one slot (cell `C5`, Tuesday periods 1–4) hosts
   *新时代中国特色社会主义理论与实践* in weeks 11–18 and *自然辩证法概论* in weeks 2–5 and 7–10. A check using
   only weekday and period calls that a clash; only the week range shows it is not.

**The workaround and its cost.** Read the weekly worksheets across the semester by eye and re-check the whole
grid whenever anything moves. Between the two saved versions 22 cell positions were dropped and 45 added with
nothing edited in place: the plan is re-laid-out, and each re-layout forces a full re-check.

**The intervention and its value measure.** Assemble one frozen input by joining the sources on course code;
detect three-dimensional clashes; and emit **two lists** — a conflict-free plan and a grab order ranked by
deferability and seat scarcity (see `doc/priority-rules.md`). Value is measured on the same input with the
same procedure: one full manual pass takes **[FILL: \_\_ minutes, stopwatched, \_\_ sections]** today and must
fall below five minutes.

**What is uncertain.** (i) Whether the semester of the missing courses can be recovered at all, or whether it
must be entered by hand once per semester — this shapes the whole design. (ii) How common the pain is beyond
one account (E1 is one person's record plus one classmate's account). (iii) Whether the empty capacity cells
in the official workbooks mean "no limit" or "not published" — currently treated as unknown, never as unlimited.

**Why this is semester-sized and not a one-hour script.** Four things carry the weight: joining three sources
that overlap partially, and doing it reproducibly; getting the three-dimensional clash rule correct and
testable; separating the two orderings (plan vs grab order) so the tool does not answer one question with the
other; and making rejections legible enough that a human will trust the output.

## 6. Scenarios

**Normal.** At the start of a season the author joins the sources to the Yuquanlu subset, checks conflicts,
ranks the plan, and works down the grab order inside the window.

**Boundary.** A part-semester course sharing a slot with a full-semester course (the measured `C5` case); two
week ranges that touch but do not overlap (weeks 3–5 and 6–8 are **not** a clash); a course at exactly
capacity; a listing captured a week ago; a course sitting exactly on the code rule (position 18 = `Y`); and a
course present in the plan that appears in no official workbook.

**Abuse.** The most obvious abuse is turning this into an **automatic course-grabbing program**: it would break
first-come-first-served fairness and would likely violate the platform's selection rules, so it is explicitly
excluded. Others: reading another student's enrolment records; fabricating section data to make a plan look
feasible; presenting an unfrozen snapshot as current; and treating an empty capacity cell as "unlimited seats"
rather than "unknown".

## 7. Semester scope

- **In scope**: join the sources on course code into one frozen input; detect weekday x period x week-range
  clashes; rank candidates by the stated priority rules; emit a plan **and** a separate grab order; explain each
  rejection by naming the overlapping week range.
- **Non-goals**: no automatic submission or any write access to the selection system; no graduation-audit rules;
  no scraping of enrolment lists; no attempt to infer the degree-programme requirement automatically.
- **Assumptions**: the sources stay obtainable so a frozen snapshot with a hash is possible; week ranges are
  integer intervals, possibly with gaps; the must-take set is supplied by a human and is not derived.
- **Stop conditions**: if a course's semester cannot be recovered from any source and cannot be supplied by
  hand, stop and report it rather than assuming the course is postponable. If the public listing ever stops
  publishing the week/period fields, narrow the scope to a timetable-consistency checker (still
  three-dimensional) rather than substituting guessed data.
